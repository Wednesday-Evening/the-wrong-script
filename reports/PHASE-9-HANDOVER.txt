2. THE STORY - THE RETURNS HATCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Route:        /stories/the-returns-hatch/
Data:         stories/data/the-returns-hatch.js
Save key:     wrong-script.story.the-returns-hatch.v1
Start scene:  hatch          Budget: 4 hours      Scenes: 10
Endings:      5 - end_filed, end_taken, end_rewritten, end_note, end_early

Premise: a night in a closed lending library. A returned card does not match
the ledger, and the borrower's record has been rewritten by someone with a key.
The reader works a four-hour night: the ledger, the stacks, the caretaker
Ivor, the back office. Each hour is spent by choosing, and the night ends
whether or not the reader is ready.

Why it is a separate experience, not a reskin of The Ninth Bell: different
premise, different setting, different cast, different clock (a night of library
hours rather than four lighthouse watches), and a different shape of choice -
the pressure here is that records can be altered, so the reader is deciding
what to believe as much as what to do.

Routes and structure:
  - Static choices plus two build-generated scenes (the night's hub and the
    closing scene), which is why the story verifier evaluates those scenes
    rather than reading a static list.
  - Five endings reachable by different combinations of discoveries, and one
    of them reachable by deliberately ending the night early.
  - Restart and a return to the library are present on the page; the engine
    handles both, so the story adds no engine logic of its own.

Content safety: no romance plot, no graphic violence, no personal data
requests, no external assets, no audio.

3. THE GAME - LAMPLIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Route:        /arcade/lamplight/
Core rules:   assets/js/lamplight-core.js
Page logic:   assets/js/lamplight.js
Levels:       arcade/data/lamplight-levels.js (data-driven)
Save key:     wrong-script.arcade.lamplight.v1

Rules: a lamp that cannot move sends one beam in a fixed direction. Mirrors
have two positions; turning one changes the exit direction. The beam crosses
windows and lights them, and stops on a wall or at the edge of the room. A room
is finished when every window in it has been lit at least once. No timer, no
move limit, no score, no failure loop.

Six rooms, in order:
  1. The Reading Room   - one mirror, one window: teaches the beam
  2. The Long Corridor  - two mirrors, two windows
  3. The Stacks         - shelves as walls, staggered mirrors
  4. The Annex          - three windows, longer routing
  5. The Lamp Room      - five mirrors, the beam crosses its own row twice
  6. The Signal Room    - six mirrors, the full length of the room

Controls: click or tap a mirror, or focus it and press Enter or Space. Room
navigation, "turn this room back", a per-room reset, and a reset-progress
control with an explicit confirm step. Progress stores unlocked and completed
rooms plus best turn counts, and is validated on read; a malformed value is
left in place and the game starts safely in memory rather than erasing it.

8. FAILURES FOUND AND FIXED DURING THIS PHASE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recorded because they are part of the audit trail, not hidden behind the final
green run.

F-1  Lamplight level data was wrong in two places.
     The Stacks row 0 was eight characters wide in a seven-wide grid, so the
     room failed to parse. The Signal Room's stored answer ended with the wrong
     mirror position, so its own answer did not light it. Both were corrected
     in the data; the rules code was not changed to accommodate them.

F-2  verify-lamplight's "rotating a mirror changes what the beam reaches" check
     asserted that one turn must NOT solve room 1. Room 1 is a one-mirror room
     and is meant to solve in one turn, so the assertion contradicted the
     design. Rewritten to assert the behaviour that actually matters: the room
     does not start solved, one turn solves it, and turning back unsolves it.

F-3  verify-story reported three unreachable scenes and only three of five
     endings. Two separate causes, both in the tool:
       - gated choices were compared as `requires[flag] === maskBit`, and the
         data writes 1/0 while the mask test yields a boolean, so every gated
         choice was rejected; fixed by comparing booleans;
       - the walk ignored the engine rule at assets/js/story-engine.js:337-341
         that sends a spent night from the tower to the water; the walk now
         mirrors that redirect, and evaluates build-generated choices in the
         state the player would be in rather than as a union of branches.
     After the fix all ten scenes and all five endings are reachable. The story
     data was not altered to make the tool pass.

F-4  verify-all failed once (11 of 12) because verify-archive-ui pinned the
     Lobby eyebrow to "Phase 7", which Phase 9 legitimately changed to
     "Phase nine - two stories, two games". The assertion was updated to check
     the current milestone. Re-run: 12 of 12.

F-5  The first browser check failed on an ambiguous locator: step 8 targeted
     '[aria-live], .status, .hud', which matched two elements. The room had in
     fact solved. This was a harness defect, not an application defect; the
     retry used the single '.hud' node and passed, with every original step and
     interaction preserved.
