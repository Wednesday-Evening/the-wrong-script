8. THE FOUR AREAS AND THEIR CONTENT
===============================================================================

A. COMPLETED FILES (3)

  1. The Ninth Bell — /stories/the-ninth-bell/
     A branching story at a lighthouse, told across four watches. Five
     endings. The card states the shape, not the conditions.

  2. The Wrong Script: Original Production — /film/
     The original cinematic production: three distinct endings and a
     post-credits scene. Filed as its own room, never as a Stories entry.

  3. SIGNAL PATH — /arcade/signal-path/
     Six handcrafted grid levels with a real completion point and replay.
     The card does not imply an endless game.

  Each card carries: room, form, shape (e.g. "five endings, four watches"),
  a short summary, a few plain facts, and one link into the experience.

B. COLLECTIONS (3)

  1. Choices and Consequences
     The Ninth Bell (choices with lasting flags and five endings), the
     Original Production (three routes out), SIGNAL PATH (movement, undo and
     the deliberate absence of a timer).

  2. Close Observation
     Gallery exhibits ledger-of-small-hours, instructions-for-a-door and
     the-understudy-note, plus the film's crew lounge, which only completes
     once all four crew members have been met.

  3. Rooms and Doors
     Gallery exhibits red-room-no-take, set-dressing-study-iii and the-booth,
     plus the film's hub of investigation rooms and the Room 13 reference
     with its painting callback.

  Every collection entry names the room, links to the real destination, and
  says why it belongs.

C. DISCOVERIES (8)

  1. Three Ways Out                The film has three distinct endings.
  2. One More Scene                A post-credits scene follows leaving.
  3. The Crew Lounge               Completes only after all four crew members.
  4. Room 13                       The reference and its painting callback.
  5. Five Hours, Five Endings      SPOILER — inside a closed disclosure.
  6. An Early Ending Is Still an Ending
                                   The story can end early and still conclude.
  7. The Closed Beacon             SIGNAL PATH refuses the beacon while
                                   fragments remain, and says why.
  8. Not Everything Is What It Appears
                                   A photograph never shot with a camera and
                                   a music exhibit that is a written cue.

  Discovery 5 is the only one with a spoiler. Its summary reads
  "Spoiler — The Ninth Bell"; the body names the hopeful ending and the two
  discoveries it requires. The title, the note and the room label contain no
  part of the answer, and the disclosure is closed on load.

D. THE RECORD AND THE CLOSING NOTE

  The Record is four short paragraphs: what is filed and why (a real
  conclusion, or a permanent place in the universe), what the collections do,
  what the discoveries are for, and what the Archive deliberately does not
  record. The closing note explains that the Archive sits alongside the other
  rooms, keeps no personal progress and collects no visitor activity.

  Attribution throughout is The Wrong Script Studio only. No individual
  authors, editors or members are invented.

9. READ-ONLY BEHAVIOUR AND WHAT IS NOT IMPLEMENTED
===============================================================================

  - No forms, no inputs, no posting, no commenting, no reactions, no voting,
    no ratings, no uploads, no submissions, no profiles, no accounts.
  - No storage of any kind. The Archive has no save key.
  - No backend, API, analytics or third-party request.
  - No fabricated dates, awards, achievements, attendance, member identities
    or popularity figures.
  - No search and no filters. The room is small enough to read, and a filter
    would have implied a bigger catalogue than exists.
  - No dead controls: the only interactive elements are links, the small-screen
    navigation toggle, the jump list and the native disclosure.

16. HISTORICAL FAILURES AND RESOLUTIONS
===============================================================================

All failures below are recorded as they happened. None was hidden, and none is
an open defect.

A. Static run 1 — 4 of 34 checks failed.
   Cause: four assertions were written too broadly, not four defects.
     1. "no other discovery... leaks the spoiler" flagged the word "Ledger".
        The Gallery's public exhibit "Ledger of Small Hours" legitimately
        carries that name; the story ending of the same name is a different
        thing. The check now excludes that one exhibit entry and still fails
        if the ending is named anywhere outside the disclosure.
     2. "never uses innerHTML" flagged the phrase inside a source comment.
        The check now strips comments and looks for real injection
        (innerHTML assignment, insertAdjacentHTML, outerHTML).
     3. "no fabricated counts" flagged the word "visitor" in honest prose
        ("not a record of visitors"). It now looks for an actual number
        attached to a metric.
     4. "mobile-first styles" flagged min-width media queries because the
        pattern matched the "width:" inside "min-width:". It now matches only
        a bare width declaration and allows up to 480px.
   Resolution: assertions corrected; no application file changed.

B. Static run 2 — 1 of 34 failed.
   Cause: the word "award" in the Record's honest sentence "no awards, no
   achievements, no attendance figures". Resolution: the check now looks for
   claims ("award-winning", "won an award", "prize", "winner", "attended",
   "trending") rather than the word itself. No application change.

C. Final static run — 34 of 34 passed.

D. Browser suite: two selectors were corrected before the first run, so no
   browser run was wasted on a known-wrong locator: the Gallery panel is
   #exhibit-panel (not a class), and the spoiler assertions had to be scoped
   to the one discovery rather than the first .discovery-note on the page.
   No failed browser run is claimed or omitted — the first executed run
   passed in full.
